The State of My Projects, November 2026

Published 2026-11-12 · last edited 2026-11-12 · 971 words · ~5 min read

Hi everyone!! It has been almost a solid 2 months since the last update to my portfolio, and I know that you missed me very very much. There are a few new projects in the Projects section. I want to take a minute to talk about these.

So, I recently got my hands on a Mac Studio — originally for local LLMs — and it has been unsurprisingly FUN to play with regarding everything. I’ll first give an overview of everything in the projects section so far, and then I’ll ask some questions I anticipate getting.

The core idea in these early projects is that I want to set up a suite of applications to serve as the backbone of all future projects. Thinking from first principles, for any application I’d want:

  1. Some centralized way to call LLMs so I don’t have to maintain different providers, logging, and versions for each different application
  2. Analytic information to see how people are using it and where to improve
  3. A way to keep track of the state of each application

And the three projects I describe next each achieve one of these goals. The projects that follow build on top of that foundation.

The first project on here is Orchestra, an agent orchestration platform that I want to be the centerpiece of agentic AI in my own projects. It’s my most involved project, but it’s also been my most neglected project recently. Why? Because I realized, soon after building it, that there is a lot of OTHER cool stuff beyond LLMs you can do with an ultra-powerful computer that never sleeps.

And while Orchestra will remain a centerpiece in my future efforts (because a lot of the cool stuff becomes COOLER once you realize that you have an intelligent-seeming little person inside your computer at your beck and call without the psychological block of worrying about API costs or a token limit), I’m reminded of one of my philosophies: that computer science and software engineering as a field began as a way to better humanity.

While some developments — like the agentic web — promote designing websites and services meant to be used by machines and computers (which I support in its own right), the end goal of all this is to better humanity in some way. Otherwise, what’s the point? So yes, AI, LLMs, and Orchestra will remain a centerpiece in all my future efforts, but it won’t be JUST AI that I care about.

One of the main design principles around Orchestra is tracking & provenance. Really, I spent an obscene amount of time designing how to log & track conversations, sessions, and the whole shebang. My logic is that since Orchestra is designed to be the central interface for LLMs my apps use, over time I will collect many many trajectories, and using those trajectories I can fine-tune or build better models for my apps and future endeavors.

Of course, this is a very long-term effort, and who knows if it will pay off? But I would rather build this feature out early than look back three months from now and realize that I could’ve had the exact data I needed to make it better.

The analytics equivalent of Orchestra is Catseye. Named after the eye of a cat (yeah I’m not that good at names), it serves as the centralized analytics data-ingestion point for my applications. Connected to it is Monitor Hub, which serves this data in a human-friendly frontend. Together, these three applications make up the core of the HBS — Hanmo Business Suite. Most of the following apps use this structure:

                      LLM / Agent data -> Orchestra
Frontend -> Server -> App Data         -> The App Itself
                      Usage data       -> Catseye -> Monitor Hub

Most files live as either SQLite DBs or JSON files because, frankly, I do not need to build at scale yet. I’ll confidently cross that bridge when I get there.

A few running apps are Pixel Game, Loveletter, and TextBot. These are mostly for fun and not too technically complex, so I’ll let you explore the publicly available ones yourself!!

As for future ambitions — I want to explore building products that can connect people, improve productivity, solve real problems, improve people’s lives, and go deeper into LLMs and agents. I have some ideas involving always-on agents, persistent state agents, games, productivity tools, fine-tuning, and other funsies that I look forward to building out bit by bit when I get some more time.
